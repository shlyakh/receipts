The grid

Electricity has to be forecast before it is made

Power cannot be stored cheaply at grid scale, so supply must match demand every hour. Grid operators decide days ahead which power plants will run. They decide from a forecast of demand, called load.

Seven regional operators run the wholesale grid for most of the United States. PJM is the largest: about 65 million people across 13 states and Washington DC.

A forecast that is too low means expensive last-minute power. One that is too high means plants paid to stand by. A few percent of error is real money, which is why every new forecasting model gets attention.

Map shaded by state for readability. Real operator territories cut across state lines: PJM also covers parts of Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Kentucky, North Carolina and Tennessee.

Our benchmark

Two PJM zones that behave differently

PJM is split into about twenty delivery zones. We forecast two of them, one hour at a time, a full week ahead.

JC, Jersey Central, New Jersey. Average load; peak . Residential, driven by summer heat.
DOM, Dominion, Virginia. Average load; peak . Large and growing, with heavy data-centre demand.

Temperature is the main driver in both, so every model gets the same temperature: the average of three weather stations per zone (the dots). We test twice, once with the weather that actually happened and once with the forecast that was available at the time.

Load: EIA hourly data. Weather: Open-Meteo. States are shaded; each zone covers part of its state, and zone boundaries are not drawn in this prototype.

The methods

What is being compared, in plain terms

How error is measured

For each of 48 test weeks, the average gap in megawatts between forecast and actual over the 168 hours.

How two methods are compared

One method's average error divided by the other's. 0.73 means 27% less error.

How sure we are

The 48 weeks are reshuffled 2,000 times to see how far the ratio moves by chance. The range shown holds 90% of those results.

What counts as a win

Fixed before scoring: the whole range must be more than 10% better. Otherwise it is a tie or undecided.

The receipt

A study said the model and the regression tie. Does that hold here?

A pre-registered study of Great Britain's demand, published this week, found that once both get the same weather, Chronos-2's error is 0.97 of a regression's, a rough tie. We ran the same question on PJM, a week ahead, on weeks the model cannot have seen in training.

Error ratio: the model's average error divided by the regression's. Below 1 the model is better. The shaded band is our 10% bar, fixed before scoring: inside it the two are a practical tie. Whiskers are 90% ranges. Source of the claim: Zenodo record 24161799.

A second check: against the field's standard benchmark

After scoring, we searched review papers and forecasting competitions for the load-forecasting field's own standard baseline. It is a different regression, known as Tao's Vanilla Benchmark: no lagged load, a curved temperature response that varies by month and hour, two years of history, plus a trend. It beats our original regression by about 20% in both zones.

Chronos-2 with temperature divided by the Vanilla benchmark with trend, forecast weather, 90% ranges. Added as a secondary result under protocol-v1.1 and scored once. The verdicts above are unchanged because their baseline was fixed in advance, but against the standard benchmark the tie reported for Great Britain largely reappears.

Why trust it

The rules were fixed before the scores existed

  • Frozen protocol. Weeks, baseline, the 10% bar and the wording of each verdict were tagged before any scored run.
  • Scored once. Each model ran once on the scored weeks; debugging used separate earlier weeks.
  • Same inputs. Every model sees the same load history and the same temperature.
  • Unseen weeks stated. Each row says how many weeks start after the model's weights were published.
  • A transfer test, not a verdict on the study. The study forecast one day ahead with more weather inputs; we forecast a week ahead with temperature only.
